The TENSA Engineering ecosystem.

TENSA Engineering organizes the public-facing explanation of NeuroCore, Argus ACLI, and Argus Lab. Each project has a different role, but they share the same foundation: persistent understanding, grounded system awareness, and controlled AI interaction.

System model

Platform. Product. Training and validation environment.

The ecosystem is intentionally layered. NeuroCore provides the platform and control architecture. Argus ACLI is the first practical product built on top of that platform. Argus Lab is the planned real-Linux environment for training users and validating diagnostic intelligence against known failure scenarios.

Project roles

Three systems, one direction.

Each project has a clear responsibility. The goal is to keep the public story understandable without blurring the technical boundaries between the platform, the product layer, and the future lab environment.

Platform

NeuroCore

NeuroCore is the local-first AI platform layer. It is designed to preserve continuity, govern tool interaction, structure system data, and provide controlled pathways between AI reasoning and real systems.

Explore NeuroCore →
Product / Distribution

Argus ACLI

Argus ACLI is the first practical product built on NeuroCore. It is a read-only Linux system intelligence tool that turns real telemetry into findings, severity, recommendations, and raw evidence.

Future Lab

Argus Lab

Argus Lab is the planned real-Linux troubleshooting and validation environment. It will use controlled faults, resettable scenarios, and mentor-style AI guidance to build and test real troubleshooting skill.

Boundary rule

The website explains. The repositories prove.

TENSA Engineering is the public explanation layer. It organizes the ideas, teaches the ecosystem, and helps people understand the direction. The GitHub repositories remain the source of truth for implementation, architecture docs, build logs, and technical history.

NeuroCore governs

The platform controls runtime flow, tool access, execution boundaries, and system interaction.

Argus explains

The product layer turns Linux system signals into clear diagnostic understanding.

Argus Lab validates

The future lab provides real failure scenarios for training users and testing diagnostic behavior.
